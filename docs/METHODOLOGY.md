# Methodology

## Operating forecast
Gross potential income equals gross leasable area multiplied by base rent and other income per square foot. Vacancy loss is deducted from gross potential income. Operating expenses are calculated per square foot. Net operating income equals effective gross income less operating expenses.

## Valuation
The income approach divides Year 1 NOI by the underwriting capitalization rate. The comparable-sales approach averages adjusted price per square foot across five fictional transactions and multiplies the average by the subject property's area. Debt sizing uses the lower valuation.

## Loan sizing
The model calculates four capacities: requested loan amount; underwritten value multiplied by maximum LTV; NOI divided by minimum DSCR and the annual mortgage constant; and NOI divided by minimum debt yield. The recommended loan is the lowest capacity rounded down to the specified loan increment.

## Interest rate and amortization
The note rate is expressed as benchmark rate plus credit spread. Monthly principal and interest payments use a fixed note rate and 25-year amortization. The five-year schedule produces a balloon balance at maturity.

## Refinance analysis
Year 5 NOI is capitalized at the assumed exit cap rate to estimate maturity value. Maturity LTV equals the balloon balance divided by that value. Refinance DSCR divides Year 5 NOI by debt service on the balloon using the assumed refinance rate and amortization. These are simplified stress indicators, not a commitment that refinancing will be available.

## Lender return
Lender IRR uses the initial net funding outflow, an assumed upfront origination fee, monthly contractual payments and repayment of the modeled balloon at maturity. It assumes no default, workout, prepayment, servicing costs or loss severity and should not be read as a probability-weighted return.

## Sensitivity analysis
The dashboard varies vacancy, benchmark rate, credit spread, underwriting cap rate, exit cap rate and refinance rate. Automated tests verify expected directions—for example, higher credit spread lowers DSCR capacity and a higher exit cap rate raises maturity LTV. These are deterministic scenarios, not probability-weighted forecasts.
